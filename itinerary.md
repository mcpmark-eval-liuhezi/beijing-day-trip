# Beijing One-Day Sightseeing Itinerary

**For:** First-time visitor with car + driver
**Base:** Hotel in the Wangfujing area, central Beijing
**Sights:** Forbidden City → Temple of Heaven → Summer Palace

---

## Route Order (and why)

The recommended order is **Hotel → Forbidden City → Temple of Heaven → Summer Palace → Hotel**.

Two things drive this order:

1. **Geography.** The Forbidden City is barely 1 km from Wangfujing and doesn't open until 8:30 a.m., so it makes a natural first stop. From there, Temple of Heaven is the short hop south, and the Summer Palace is the long haul out to Haidian in the northwest — a run that ends the day right back toward the hotel.
2. **Driving time.** This order avoids the worst leg of the day. Coming home from Temple of Heaven (the alternative ordering) means fighting 5.8 km of central-Beijing traffic through Dongcheng back to Wangfujing at the end of a long day; returning from the Summer Palace instead rides the ring roads. The numbers below show the day's legs.

> **Does reordering save real driving time?** Only a little. Every ordering of the three sites comes out within roughly 3–4 km of every other, so the sequence can't be chosen for distance alone. The recommended order is the practical winner: it gets the huge site (the Forbidden City) done early while energy is high, puts the relaxing lakeside walk of the Summer Palace at the end of the day, and keeps the heaviest central-city traffic leg off the evening.

## Stop-by-Stop Plan

### Stop 1 — Forbidden City (Palace Museum), Dongcheng District

**Drive from hotel:** 0.9 km, about 5–6 minutes.

**Plan:** Enter from the north (Jingshan) side if your driver approaches from Jingshan Front Street, or from the Meridian Gate (south entrance) after being dropped at the east gate — check with the driver which side is easiest on the day. Walk the central axis: the Meridian Gate, the Hall of Supreme Harmony, the Hall of Central Harmony, the Hall of Preserving Harmony, then the Inner Court's Palace of Heavenly Purity, the Hall of Union, and the Palace of Earthly Tranquility, exiting through the Gate of Divine Prowess at the north. Allow 2.5–3 hours. (The Palace Museum is closed Mondays — plan the day accordingly.)

**History (read in the car):** The Forbidden City is the imperial palace complex at the heart of Beijing, built on the orders of the Yongle Emperor after he moved the Ming capital from Nanjing to Beijing. Construction ran from 1406 to 1420, took 14 years, and required more than a million workers. It served as the seat of 24 emperors — 14 Ming and 10 Qing — until 1924, making it the political center of China for over five centuries. Today it's administered as the Palace Museum (established 1925) and holds UNESCO World Heritage status (1987) as the largest preserved royal palace complex still standing in the world.

---

### Stop 2 — Temple of Heaven, Dongcheng District

**Drive from Forbidden City:** 5.5 km, about 19 minutes.

**Plan:** Have the driver drop you at the north or east gate. Walk the park's north–south axis: the Hall of Prayer for Good Harvests (the triple-gabled blue-roofed icon), the Imperial Vault of Heaven with its Echo Wall, then down the Vermilion Steps Bridge to the Circular Mound Altar at the south end. Allow 1.5–2 hours; the surrounding park is worth strolling — locals use it for morning exercise and choral singing, and it's a genuinely lovely place to linger.

**History (read in the car):** The Temple of Heaven was first built in 1420 under the same Yongle Emperor who built the Forbidden City, originally as a combined Altar of Heaven and Earth; it was renamed the Temple of Heaven in 1534 under the Jiajing Emperor, and its present layout was largely fixed by Qing reconstruction completed around 1749. Ming and Qing emperors came here twice a year — most importantly at the winter solstice — to pray to Heaven for good harvests, in ceremonies the emperor had to complete perfectly, since the smallest mistake was thought to bode ill for the whole nation. UNESCO inscribed it in 1998, calling it a masterpiece of architecture and landscape design whose symbolic layout influenced building and planning across the Far East for centuries.

---

### Stop 3 — Summer Palace, Haidian District

**Drive from Temple of Heaven:** 26.2 km, about 35 minutes.

**Plan:** Enter via the East Palace Gate or the North Palace Gate. A classic route: Longevity Hill's front-hill halls and pavilions (Tower of Buddhist Incense at the top), then the 700+ meter Long Corridor along the north shore of Kunming Lake, out across the Seventeen-Arch Bridge to Nanhu Island, and back. Allow 2–2.5 hours. If legs are tired by now, the lake boat rides are a scenic shortcut.

**History (read in the car):** The Summer Palace (Yiheyuan) sits on land used as an imperial pleasure ground since the Jin and Ming dynasties, but its core was created by the Qianlong Emperor, who from 1749 expanded the Western Lake into Kunming Lake, used the excavated earth to enlarge and rename the hill as Longevity Hill, and completed the "Garden of Clear Ripples" by 1764 at a cost of over 4.8 million silver taels. Anglo-French forces sacked and burned the gardens in 1860 during the Second Opium War; Empress Dowager Cixi rebuilt and enlarged it between 1884 and 1895, giving it its present name in 1888. UNESCO designated it a World Heritage Site in 1998, and it covers 2.9 square kilometers — about three-quarters of it water.

### Return — Hotel, Wangfujing

**Drive from Summer Palace:** 27.3 km, about 43 minutes.

## Practical Notes

- **Forbidden City closed Mondays.** Book tickets in advance through the Palace Museum.
- **Summer Palace is huge** — 2.9 km², three-quarters of it water. Don't try to see everything; the Long Corridor + Seventeen-Arch Bridge loop is the classic.
- **Timing:** with ~6 hours on-site across the three sites plus ~1 hr 45 min driving, this is a full 8 a.m.–5/6 p.m. day. Starting at the Forbidden City's opening time is strongly advised.
- **Feet, not wheels:** all three sites are walk-only inside the gates — the car is only for the hops between them.
